# Reference products

Learn from reference products by **what they teach**, not by how they look. Copying a surface style (Linear's dark UI, Stripe's gradients) produces a generic imitation. Borrowing a lesson (how Linear keeps a dense tool calm, how Stripe explains money) produces better work that is still your own.

These are starting points chosen for durable reasons. Products change, so check the current version before quoting a specific screen, and prefer the person's own references (in `DESIGN-CONTEXT.md`) over this list.

## Designing for real life

| Product | Lesson |
|---|---|
| Google Maps (navigation mode) | Strips the interface down to what's needed while moving: large targets, glanceable information, voice as a first-class channel |
| Uber / ride-hailing apps | Status is always visible; the user never wonders what's happening or how long it will take |
| Apple Wallet / boarding passes | Surfaces the right thing at the right moment and place with no navigation |
| Citymapper-style transit apps | Plans for failure: delays, alternatives, "what if I miss this" |

## Clarity in complex domains

| Product | Lesson |
|---|---|
| Wise | Makes fees, rates and timing explicit before you commit; honesty as a design feature |
| Revolut | Dense financial capability made approachable through progressive disclosure |
| Stripe (dashboard and docs) | Complex systems explained in layers; excellent empty, error and test states |
| GOV.UK Design System | Plain language, one thing per page, patterns backed by research with real users, accessibility as a baseline |

## Calm and focus

| Product | Lesson |
|---|---|
| Linear | A dense tool that stays calm: restrained colour, keyboard-first speed, one clear focus per view |
| Things 3 | Delight through restraint; very few elements, each carefully considered |
| iA Writer | Removes everything that isn't the task |

## Restrained, functional motion

| Product | Lesson |
|---|---|
| iOS system apps | Motion explains spatial relationships (where a sheet came from, where an item went) and is quick |
| Material motion guidance | A documented rationale for when motion helps, with durations and easing |

## Brand character and expressive web

| Product | Lesson |
|---|---|
| Stripe (marketing site) | Explains a complex product with interactive, animated diagrams that teach rather than decorate |
| Duolingo | A character and colour system strong enough to be recognized from any single screen |
| Monzo | One signature colour and a plain, warm voice carry the brand everywhere |
| Headspace | An illustration system that makes an abstract product (calm) visible and friendly |
| Apple product pages | One product story told through scroll, with the product itself as the hero |
| Airbnb | Photography and real content as the brand; the product is the places |

Borrow the principle (a system of character, interactive explanation, product as hero), never the specific look.

## System craft

| System | Lesson |
|---|---|
| Material Design 3 | Tokens, adaptive layouts and accessibility guidance at scale |
| Apple Human Interface Guidelines | Platform conventions and why they exist |
| Shopify Polaris | Content guidelines and merchant-focused patterns documented alongside components |
| Atlassian Design System | Governance and contribution for many teams |
| GOV.UK Design System | How to document patterns with evidence of what was tested |

## How to cite a reference

Write "Borrow from [product]: [the lesson], applied here as [the specific decision]." For example: "Borrow from Wise: show the full cost before commit, applied here as the delivery date and fee on the review screen, not after payment."
