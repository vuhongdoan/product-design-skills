# Marketing sites and landing pages

A marketing page has seconds to answer: what is this, is it for me, why should I care, what do I do next? And it has to be remembered afterward. Most visitors scan on a phone.

## Before designing

Brand-facing pages need brand input. Ask the intake questions in `creative-direction.md` (positioning, existing brand, personality, competitors, taste, boldness) unless you already have them. Look at what competitors and category leaders do now, and decide whether to fit the category or break from it.

## The first screen

- Says **what it is and who it's for** in a few plain words. A strong line can have personality; it must not need decoding.
- **Shows** the product, its result or its world: a hero object, a playable slice, a striking image or a type treatment that is the identity. Not a paragraph, not an abstract gradient.
- One primary call to action, with the same label everywhere on the page.
- Loads fast on a mobile network. Heavy 3D or video needs a light fallback.

## Structure

- One idea per section, ordered by a sceptical visitor's questions: what, for whom, how it works, proof, cost, the catch, how to start.
- **Each section leads with something to look at or try**, with copy supporting it. Aim for a visual or interactive anchor in every section.
- Vary the rhythm: full-bleed colour blocks, a big visual, a compact FAQ. Not the same heading-plus-paragraph layout repeated down the page.
- Let sections breathe: generous space between sections (at least 64 px on mobile, 96 px on desktop) and plenty of room around the hero's one idea. Space is a large part of what makes a page feel confident; a crowded page reads as cheap. See `standards/whitespace.md`.
- Proof is specific and honest: real customers, real numbers with context, real quotes with permission. Never invent testimonials; if there's no proof yet, show the product instead.
- Pricing is clear, with the full cost visible before sign-up.

## Visual direction

- Take the identity from the concept (see `creative-direction.md`): place, materials, rituals, vocabulary, the product's own artifacts.
- Use characterful type, confident colour and real imagery. Default system fonts, grey palettes and hairline-rule layouts read as unbranded or dated on a marketing page.
- Use current trends where they fit the concept (see `trends.md`): kinetic or oversized type, saturated colour, interactive demos, 3D product moments, collage or texture.
- One signature element people will remember.

## Interaction

Include at least one interaction that lets visitors experience the value: a calculator, configurator, before/after, a live slice of the product, a map or time picker. It should teach something about the product, not just move.

## Motion

- One signature moment (the hero, the demo or one scroll story), plus responsive feedback on interactive elements.
- No fade-in on every section, no looping pulses, no scroll-jacking by default.
- Reduced motion shows a still equivalent.

## Copy

- Specific beats impressive. "A mechanic at your door, once a month" beats "Elevate your cycling journey".
- Short. Headlines of about 3–8 words; supporting lines under ~25 words.
- The CTA says what happens next ("Check my address", "Start free trial").

## Marketing anti-patterns

- Vague hero headline and a stock gradient
- Text walls: sections of paragraphs and bullet lists with nothing to see or try
- An unbranded page: system font, grey palette, no mark, no imagery
- Logo walls from companies that don't use the product
- Countdown timers and fake scarcity
- Pop-ups before the visitor has read anything
- Footers with forty links
