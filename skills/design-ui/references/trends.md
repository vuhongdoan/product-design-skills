# Current design trends

**Snapshot as of September 2026.** Trends move fast. Before starting new UI or brand-facing work, and when you have web access, spend a few minutes checking what's current: search for this year's UI and web design trends for the product's category, and look at 3–5 current category leaders and recent award-winning sites (Awwwards, Godly, Mobbin, Land-book, Dribbble shots from studios you trust). Update this snapshot in your notes with what you find and the date.

## How to use trends

Trends are vocabulary, not direction. They make work feel current, and they date quickly when used as decoration. Run each one through the **fit test** before using it:

1. **Concept**: does it express this product's idea, or would it look the same on any brand?
2. **Audience and context**: does it suit the people and their situation? A calm, anxious-user fintech flow and a Gen Z event launch want opposite things.
3. **Budgets**: does it keep one focal point, clear comprehension and purposeful motion?
4. **Cost**: performance on a mid-range phone and slow network, accessibility (contrast, motion, readability), build effort.
5. **Shelf life**: will it look dated in 18 months? Use fast-moving trends in replaceable layers (a campaign page, an illustration), not in the core system.

Aim to feel **of this moment** without being a trend collage: pick one or two that fit the concept and execute them well.

## Platform design languages (native apps)

For native apps, the current platform language is not optional: people learn it from every other app on their phone.

- **iOS: Liquid Glass** (introduced with iOS 26 in 2025). Controls, bars and sheets are translucent, layered and float over content; content runs edge to edge beneath them; shapes are rounder and concentric with the device corners. Use system materials rather than faking blur; keep text on glass legible (Apple added a less-transparent option for users).
- **Android: Material 3 Expressive.** Bolder shape variety, emphasized type, richer colour with Dynamic Color from the user's wallpaper, and springier, physics-based motion. Google is also bringing Liquid Glass treatments to some of its own iOS apps, so cross-platform products increasingly follow each platform natively rather than one shared look.

## Product UI (apps, tools, dashboards)

- **Calm interfaces**: fewer decisions per screen, generous space, progressive disclosure. A response to overloaded users; fits the attention budget.
- **Transparent AI**: show why a suggestion appeared, how confident the system is, and give an easy override or undo. Show AI working (labor illusion) without fake delays.
- **Explanatory motion**: motion that shows where things came from and went, and what the system is doing. Decorative motion is out of favour in product UI.
- **Accessibility-first systems**: high-contrast modes, keyboard paths and reduced motion designed in from the start.
- **Multimodal input**: touch, keyboard, voice and gesture used interchangeably.
- **Token-driven systems**: variables for colour, type and spacing, with light/dark and brand themes swapped by token.
- **Gamified progress** (streaks, levels, rewards), increasingly questioned when it turns into pressure: use progress to motivate, never guilt (see the ethics rule).

## Brand and marketing web

- **Bold and kinetic typography**: oversized headlines, custom or characterful fonts, type as the main visual, type that moves or responds to scroll or pointer.
- **Vibrant, saturated colour**: confident palettes and high-contrast pairings replacing the muted beige-and-grey look; colour blocks as structure.
- **3D and immersive product moments**: interactive models, product explorers, WebGL scenes, AR previews. Best when it lets people inspect or try the product.
- **Interactive demos over screenshots**: live, playable product slices, calculators and configurators in the page.
- **Scroll-told narratives**: a product story that builds as you scroll. Powerful for one hero story; tiring if every section does it.
- **Expressive textures and styles**: collage and cut-out (sticker graphics, torn paper, photo cutouts), retrofuturism (chrome, neon, pixel), neo-brutalism (raw, high-contrast, visible structure) and maximalism (dense, layered, rich). Strong personality; pick only when the concept calls for it.
- **Experimental navigation**: radial menus, map-like or nonlinear journeys. Fine for portfolios and campaigns; risky for anything task-driven.
- **Sustainable web**: lean pages, optimized media, fewer heavy scripts. A quality signal that fits well with performance on mobile.

## Tensions to resolve deliberately

- **Calm vs maximal**: calm suits tools and stressful tasks; maximal suits entertainment, fashion, culture and launches. Choose from the context brief, not taste.
- **Immersive vs fast**: 3D and scroll narratives cost load time and battery. Provide a light fallback and respect reduced motion.
- **Glass vs legibility**: translucency looks current and can hurt contrast. Test text on busy backgrounds.
